The Blockchain

Blockchain technology is at the core of the service. Blockchains are decentralized public ledgers made up of digitally recorded data in a chain of packages called ‘blocks’. The way these blocks are linked is chronological, meaning that data which is finalized on a blockchain is impossible to reverse or otherwise censor once it is recorded. Since these ledgers are distributed all over the world (across several ‘nodes’ which replicate the ledger and form consensus to secure its accuracy), there is no single person or computer making decisions or otherwise administering the system (such as an operator of a cloud computing system), and there is no centralized place where it can be said to be located either.

By design, a blockchain’s records cannot be changed or deleted. All information recorded on blockchains, including any transactions you make when using them, are entirely public and immutable. This may affect your ability to exercise your rights such as your right to erasure (‘right to be forgotten’), or your rights to object or restrict processing, of your personal data. Data on the blockchain can’t be erased or changed.

In certain circumstances, to comply with our contractual obligations to you (such as delivery of tokens) it will be necessary to write certain personal data, such as your wallet address, onto the blockchain; this requires you to execute such transactions using your wallet’s private key.

In every case with respect to the App or the Services, the ultimate decision to (i) transact on the blockchain using your wallet address, as well as (ii) share the public key relating to your wallet address with anyone (including us) rests with you. If you make no transactions, then you make no publication to the blockchain.

If you want to ensure your privacy rights are not affected in any way, you should not transact on blockchains as certain rights may not be fully available or exercisable by you or us due to the fact that most blockchains and particularly the ones relevant to the App are intrinsically public.

When you make a transaction on the blockchain, including those blockchains which you may interact with using the services, you are necessarily and always publishing information publicly. All blockchain transactions are available to the public and open for audit; and all of your transactions or interactions using blockchain will become immediately and immutably publicly available.
